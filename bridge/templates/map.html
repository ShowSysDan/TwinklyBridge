1. What to map

The patterns themselves are full green (no dimming, so no flicker in the photos). The wait is measured at the start of every capture: the round trip to the bridge, plus how long the camera takes to show a change, with a margin. It is counted from sending each pattern, because the LEDs change while the request is still in flight. This box is only the minimum.

Most of a capture is spent waiting for that round trip, so connect the phone to the bridge directly rather than through a VPN: the result page reports the round trip it measured, and every 100 ms of it costs about 3 s on a 1,250-LED capture.

Darken the room if you can. Hold the phone still for each capture (about 20 s): a tripod, or lean it on something. For a flat piece one capture from the front is enough. For a 3D piece (tree, sphere) capture it from several sides, walking around it to your right, about 45° apart (8 views all round is best; 4 is the minimum), keeping the same distance and height. Stand back and zoom in rather than standing close.

Compass and tilt: allow motion access when asked.

The LEDs blink while you aim: green on the picture = LEDs the camera sees, red = too bright (clipped).

The guided walk spreads its views over the part you can reach, instead of stopping half way round a circle it assumed. Hold the phone level with the middle of the piece: if you can't raise it that high, stand further back - the tilt is an angle, so twice the distance is half the tilt.

Walk around: the phone captures a view, tells you how far to walk to your right, and captures again by itself when you're there, upright and still, until it has been all the way round the part you can reach; then builds the 3D map. For a flat piece, Capture one view is enough.
